# The Last Full Stop

*A History, Reconstructed*

A fable of the war between Letters and Numbers, and the Punctuation caught
between them — told as a historian's reconstructed archive, three centuries
after the fact, from testimony that mostly doesn't agree.

## Structure

Eleven numbered sections (I–XI) plus an Interlude, an Appendix, and four
plates, framed throughout as a historian's archive rather than a straight
narrative:

- **I–IV** — the war's origins, the Punctuation's neutral ground, the
  Question Mark, and the Comma's strike that breaks both nations' language.
- **Interlude** — a tavern ledger, the one document that survives the strike
  intact; deliberately undignified, included against the historian's own
  better judgment.
- **V–VI** — the Period, and the war's end by fiat, recorded as three
  irreconcilable eyewitness accounts.
- **VII–IX** — the Aftermath, the first open Punctuation Assembly, and the
  Era of Quiet.
- **X** — the Scribe's Note: the one claim every surviving witness agrees
  on.
- **XI** — the Last Full Stop: the Period's final, unconfirmed sighting, and
  the historian's refusal to adjudicate where the war really ended.
- **Appendix** — On the Lost Scrap: the historian's own unprovable
  conviction, offered as personal record rather than scholarship.

The book is built on a standing convention: every testimony carries a
corroboration label (uncorroborated, disputed, self-reported, corroborated
by *n* sources), and the historian never resolves a disputed account into a
single truth. The one exception is section X, which exists specifically
because it's the one thing every account agrees on.

## Reading order note

Section X (the Scribe's Note — "she only asked them to stop") was
deliberately placed *before* XI (her final sighting), so the reader meets
the tired woman on the road already knowing the one true thing about her,
rather than being told it after the fact.
